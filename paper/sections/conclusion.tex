\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have compared hand-crafted pipelines, fine-tuned CNNs and their ensembles on SkyView, holding the test set, the validation signal and the training pool fixed and varying only the class distribution.
The CNNs beat the best hand-crafted pipeline by about 12 points on balanced data and lose much less under a 16:1 long tail.
For the CNNs, re-weighting the loss was the better answer to imbalance; augmenting the minority classes helped only the hand-crafted pipelines.
Averaging the two CNNs gave the best result in every setting, while voting with much weaker models did not, and a two-member hard vote is only meaningful with an explicit tie-break.
The obvious next steps are to repeat the study over several seeds and long-tail orderings, to try a combiner learned on validation predictions, and to check whether the findings carry over to larger benchmarks such as AID and NWPU-RESISC45~\cite{xia2017aid,cheng2017remote}.
